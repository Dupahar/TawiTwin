# Tawi Twin · 3-minute demo video script

Target 2:50. Voice: Amazon Polly "Kajal" (neural, en-IN). Captions burned in (viewers may watch muted).
Every number below comes from the live system or a cited source.

| # | Scene (what is on screen) | Narration |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hook.** Intro screen: "296 mm of rain in 24 hours." Slow zoom. | On the twenty-sixth of August 2025, Jammu got two hundred and ninety-six millimetres of rain in twenty-four hours. A new record. The Tawi rose above its highest flood level, part of the Fourth Tawi Bridge gave way, and more than five thousand people were evacuated. |
| 2 | **Problem + who.** Live site, map of Jammu, risk hexagons. | Residents had no warning for their own street. The city had no list of where to send pumps or which drains to clear first. Tawi Twin is a monsoon and heat command tool for Jammu. For residents, and for the city's control room. |
| 3 | **Resident asks (replay).** Click "Replay the flood". Type "Kal Bhagwati Nagar mein paani bharega?" Map flies, pin, card, answer streams in. | Let's replay the night before the flood. A resident asks, in Hinglish: will Bhagwati Nagar flood tomorrow? The agent finds the colony, pulls the evidence, and checks the forecast. High waterlogging risk, with one hundred and eighty-five millimetres expected. It explains why: satellites saw water here, the ground is low, and news reports named this area. Then what to do, the nearest hospital, and a school on higher ground. |
| 4 | **Share + Hindi.** Click "Share on WhatsApp" (show message preview). Then Hindi question answered in Devanagari. | One tap shares the alert on WhatsApp, where Jammu actually talks. It answers in Hindi, Hinglish or English, by typing or by voice. |
| 5 | **Every building.** Buildings view, 3D, pan along the Tawi. Then Flood layer with reported-locality markers. | Underneath, every one of a hundred and eighty-eight thousand buildings is scored. How close to the river, how high above the nearest drain, and whether radar saw water beside it on the twenty-seventh of August. We checked it against the localities news reports named as flooded: buildings there are one and a half times more likely to be rated high risk than the city average. |
| 6 | **Control room.** Command tab: 39 areas, 12,113 buildings, pump positions highlighted, drains layer. | For the control room, the same data becomes a plan. Thirty-nine areas at high alert, twelve thousand buildings inside them. Where to pre-position dewatering pumps. Which drains to clear first. Which low-lying schools to check. |
| 7 | **Citizen report.** Report tab: upload waterlogging photo, location, send. AI triage card appears; report shows in Command inbox, officer marks "assigned". | Residents can report waterlogging with a photo. A vision model on Amazon Bedrock checks it, rates the severity, and it lands in the control room's inbox, ready to assign. |
| 8 | **Where AWS fits.** Architecture card. | The whole backend runs on AWS. The agent is built with Strands Agents on Amazon Bedrock. Amazon Location Service finds colonies the open maps miss. Lambda on Graviton, API Gateway, DynamoDB, and EventBridge refresh the forecast every hour. Buildings come from AWS Open Data, served through S3 and CloudFront. The whole stack is one AWS SAM template. |
| 9 | **Today + close.** Switch to Live: "No waterlogging alert, all 284 areas low." End card with URL. | And today? It's dry, and Tawi Twin says so. When the next monsoon comes, every street in Jammu will know before the water does. Tawi Twin. |
